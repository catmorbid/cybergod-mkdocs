# Atlas Global Defense (AGD)

Atlas Global Defense is a private military contractor aligned with the Commonwealth. They manage a large military-industrial complex and are involved in many military R&D projects. In addition, AGD provides a significant force of security and military personnel for a large number of sanctioned clients, most of which are private corporations or individuals. Military contracting is extremely regulated and scrutinized and with perseverance, strict code of conduct and unapologetic ruthlessness, AGD has managed to gain and hold a significant position in the field.

AGD mercenaries take any kind of contracts and unlike other factions, they are in it just for the money. They have been involved in a large number of operations for decades and while their reputation is outstanding, it is not completely impeccable, and there have been reports of unprofessional behavior, abuse of power and involvement in illegal operations. AGD attempts to maintain a public image that is clean, reliable and trustworthy, so it will take whatever means necessary to maintain this image.

#### Frame Upgrade Limitations
AGD has access to `Cybertech` augments, but Commonwealth regulations only allow **Level 2** augments to private operators. In addition, they are cleared for **Advanced grade** equipment, and may not officially obtain higher grade equipment. The AGD has found to be circumventing these regulations at times through backchannel access, or even resorting to black market and hacked or reverse-engineered schematics. AGD tend to compensate their limited access to advanced augments by improving the raw performance of their frames

### Contracts
AGD characters are bound to their client via a Contract. The [[merits]] earned on a job depend on how well they adhere to their contract. Morality is secondary, what matters is the terms laid out in the ink. Any breach of contract is an immediate demerit, but if you manage to go the extra mile to please your client, you can expect bonus merits.

To evaluate the merits, GM first presents how many merits can be gained if you pull off the job without a hitch. E.g. let’s say a small gig is worth 10 merits. You succeed and this is how much you get. You know the stakes beforehand, that’s part of the job. Needless to say, if you fail the job, you will get nothing and any demerits are subtracted from your existing tally. Yes, you can be demoted.

Then, for each *breach of contract*, GM assigns a demerit based on type of breach
- **Minor Breach (-1)** – You were supposed to deliver the package by 18:00 tuesday to the drop point. You were followed and had to get rid of the tail, and ended up getting in a firefight. You arrived at the drop point, 18:23. A bit late, but in this case, it’s understandable, albeit unfortunate.
- **Moderate Breach (-2)** – The instructions were clear: No one is to be harmed during the mission. But you ended up beating up a security guard pretty badly. The job could have failed entirely if you didn’t, you had no choice!
- **Major Breach (-5)** – The client paid for discretion. You were told to retrieve the data without alerting anyone, and certainly not to engage with local law enforcement. But instead, you left half the facility in flames and triggered an international incident. You got the data, but the fallout is immense — and expensive.
- **Severe Breach (-10)** – You were supposed to get in and out, fast, before anyone sees you. But instead you messed up, got caught, was tortured and spilled the beans. The client was compromised and had to relocate. Then you left a pile of bodies during your escape, and made several new enemies. At least you pulled off the job, by some miraculous streak of luck, no doubt. Needless to say, this client will never work with us or you again.
- **Unforgivable Breach (-20)** – The asset was not to be harmed under any circumstances. Not only did you fail to extract them, but you executed them on-site and broadcast the footage on an open channel. The client has disavowed the operation. AGD is already preparing the paperwork to terminate your contract — and possibly you.
