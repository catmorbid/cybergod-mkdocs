title: Stress

Witnessing violence, fear and otherworldly horrors puts a strain on the mind. Only the most hardened veterans become almost immune, and many claim that's the only way to know know if you're still human, machine or not.

Stress is handled different than physical damage, and instead of knives and bullets causing the harm, stress is caused by tense situations, such as social pressure, violence, mind-altering drugs, psionic resonance and witnessing otherworldly horrors.

### Stress Tables
Stress is generally measured on a scale of 1 to 5, but it does not have an upper limit. You can use the following tables to estimate how much stress is inflicted, based on the type of situation characters find themselves in.

#### Combat & Violence Stress table

|Stress|Description|Example Situation|
|---|---|---|
|1|**Minor**|Taking a grazing shot; seeing a teammate wounded; being ordered to draw on someone who hasn’t made a move yet; hearing the calm tone of someone casually executing a prisoner over comms.|
|2|**Moderate**|Being flanked with no cover; watching someone bleed out slowly; firing into a crowd to protect your team; discovering your bullets hit someone they shouldn’t have; realizing your target was just a terrified teenager.|
|3|**Major**|Killing someone up close and seeing their face twist in confusion; receiving a direct order to finish off survivors; dragging the remains of a teammate into extraction while under fire; shooting someone who was begging not to die.|
|4|**Severe**|Surviving an explosion only to realize you’re covered in your ally’s remains; being the only one left alive after a lost engagement; hearing an enemy beg for mercy in a voice that sounds like your own; carrying out a clean execution and realizing you felt nothing.|
|5|**Extreme**|Accidentally killing a civilian and having no one acknowledge it; being ordered to silence a witness and obeying; watching your team wipe out a group that had already surrendered — and being told it was “classified necessity.”|

#### Social Stress table

|Stress|Description|Example Situation|
|---|---|---|
|1|**Minor**|Being publicly reprimanded or mocked by a superior; social isolation during downtime; awkward silence after a failed persuasion attempt; witnessing a peer lie with disturbing ease.|
|2|**Moderate**|Receiving veiled threats from someone in power; confessing a mistake that nearly got someone killed; being pressured to betray a friend; realizing you’ve been manipulated for days.|
|3|**Major**|Being forced to interrogate someone you once trusted; watching a comrade’s reputation be destroyed for political reasons — and knowing the truth will get you killed; being gaslit by someone you can’t afford to doubt.|
|4|**Severe**|Testifying against an ally under oath while knowing the sentence is death; realizing someone has fabricated a false history of you — and everyone believes it; being humiliated by command in front of your squad.|
|5|**Extreme**|Being exiled or dishonorably discharged from your faction; learning your entire career was orchestrated as a social experiment; a loved one rejects you for who you've become; a public tribunal broadcasts your “sins” live — and you can’t refute them.|
#### Supernatural & Horror Stress table

| Stress | Grade        | Example Situation                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------ | ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1      | **Minor**    | Brushed by hostile intent; mild psychic residue; eerie silence before an ambush; seeing a wound that won’t stop pulsing; a synthetic muttering in a forgotten language; distant screaming on a loop.                                                                                                   |
| 2      | **Moderate** | A sudden surge of psionic feedback; being targeted by a mental probe; encountering a corpse with unnatural decomposition; friendly AI emits a sound it should not be able to make; seeing a non-Euclidean spatial fold distort a hallway in real time.                                                 |
| 3      | **Major**    | Witnessing a teammate's mind unravel mid-mission; getting lost in a memory loop that isn't your own; realizing you’ve forgotten the last 15 minutes and no one noticed; a face in the mirror keeps watching after you leave; a kill order appears in your HUD — with your name on it.                  |
| 4      | **Severe**   | Hearing your own voice over comms issuing impossible commands; being physically overwritten by a psionic echo; an ally suddenly bleeds from the eyes and doesn't stop; seeing a loved one’s face on an enemy's body; being trapped inside a looping hallucination where the team dies again and again. |
| 5      | **Extreme**  | A member of the team is forcibly converted into something else — and remains conscious; your personality begins to fragment across Cyberweb nodes; encountering a godlike entity that recognizes you by name and offers mercy; you shoot yourself to wake up — but it doesn’t work this time.          |

### Stress Check

Whenever you take Stress, mark it down on the stress tracker and make a [[resource-check|Resource Check]] with [[mental-state#Dissociation|Dissociation]], rolling dice equal to current stress. If you fail, you suffer from [[#Mental Breakdown]].

!!! info Tracking Stress
	Only major characters and player characters should roll stress dice and track stress. Minor characters should simply roll on the mental breakdown table if they take any points of stress, or GM should decide the outcome.

### Mental Breakdown

Having failed a Stress test you need to roll your [[mental-state#Habituation|Habituation]] Die to resolve outcome on mental breakdown. You can spend **Resolve** to re-roll the outcome (see [[resolve]])

| Habituation Die | Outcome                   | Details                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1               | **Jittery**               | You flinch, twitch, and overreact to everything. Until end of scene, take `DIF 1` on all `Reflex` tests. Any surprise triggers a useless Reaction (e.g. ducking, twitch fire).              |
| 2               | **Drop Reflex**           | You instinctively drop whatever you're holding — weapon, gear, data shard, whatever. If you’re holding multiple items, GM chooses what slips.                                               |
| 3               | **Verbal Overflow**       | You start compulsively narrating your thoughts or whispering aloud — even in stealth. Until resolved, take `DIF 1` on all `social` and `Stealth` tests.                                     |
| **4**           | **Flight Response**       | You must spend your next **`Action` and `Reaction`** moving away from the threat or hiding, regardless of what else is happening. You may crawl, bolt, or curl up — GM discretion.          |
| 5               | **Sensory Drift**         | Everything feels off. Sounds are too loud, colors too bright, touch too sharp. All perception or focus -based tests are made with `DIF 1`.                                                  |
| **6**           | **Panic Spiral**          | You lose grip on your surroundings. You must perform **one irrational `action`** (GM decides or offers 2-3 choices). Until resolved, all your tests are `DIF 1`.                            |
| 7               | **Motor Disruption**      | You develop shakes, stumbles, or muscle tightness. Until the scene ends, you suffer `DIF 1` on all `Body` and `Reflex` tests.                                                               |
| **8**           | **Emotional Breakdown**   | You begin sobbing, laughing, ranting, or shutting down. You **cannot spend `Teamwork Dice`** or give orders for the rest of the scene. Everyone nearby or Linked remotely takes `Stress 1`. |
| 9               | **Delusional Threats**    | You become convinced someone — maybe even a teammate — is out to get you. Until resolved, all `Mind` tests are made with `DIF 1`. Your actions reflect the paranoia.                        |
| **10**          | **Compulsive Aggression** | You must damage or destroy something nearby — gear, a wall, a hostage. You receive `DIF 1` on all `Mind` and `Social` tests for the rest of the scene.                                      |
| 11              | **Fixation Loop**         | You become obsessed with one idea, target, or goal — “must kill that guy,” “must burn the data,” “must finish the climb.” Until it's done or interrupted, you ignore other threats.         |
| **12**          | **Psychotic Break**       | You fully snap. For the rest of the scene, you attack anything within reach, including allies, unless directly restrained or sedated. Use your deadliest available weapon.                  |
### Mental Condition
If you suffer from two Mental Breakdowns during the same game session, you will take a deep long-lasting mental condition, as well as a point of [[engrams#Corruption|Corruption]]. Choose one from the table below, roll a D10, or make up something else that fits. Each Mental Condition has a beneficial effects and a downside. If you find yourself unwilling to live with your condition, you can seek professional help, and pay some [[experience#Experience Points|Experience Points]] to recover.

| D10 | Condition                      | Benefit                                                                                                                           | Drawback                                                                                                                         |
| --- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | **Flashbacks**                 | Once per session, you can recall vivid past tactical detail, gaining **D8 Advantage** on a `Tactics` or `Alertness` test.         | Specific triggers (e.g. alarms, blood) force a `Psychology+Mind` check. On failure, you **lose your next `Action`**.             |
| 2   | **Hypervigilance**             | Gain **D6 Advantage** on all `Alertness` tests while in unsafe or unfamiliar environments.                                        | You take `Stress 1` whenever the group relaxes, lingers, or drops formation.                                                     |
| 3   | **Detachment**                 | Immune to all emotional manipulation and morale penalties.                                                                        | Cannot initiate or benefit from **Teamwork Dice** unless another character makes an emotional appeal.                            |
| 4   | **Intrusive Guilt**            | Once per session, you can **automatically act first** in defense of an ally in danger.                                            | When a civilian, ally, or subordinate is harmed, take `Stress 1` and suffer `DIF 1` to Social checks for the scene.              |
| 5   | **Paranoia**                   | Gain **D6 Advantage** to detect deception or hidden threats.                                                                      | All trust-building `Social` rolls (Persuasion, Empathy, etc.) are made at `DIF 1`. You are required to question group decisions. |
| 6   | **Emotional Contagion**        | Your emotional state amplifies the room — allies gain a **D6 Advantage** on social actions if you're confident.                   | When you take `Stress`, **allies within range also take `Stress 1`** unless they succeed `Willpower`.                            |
| 7   | **Reality Bleed**              | Once per session, you can ask the GM, _"Is this real?"_ and receive a cryptic or partial truth.                                   | If you fail an `Alertness` or `Mind` test, you must act as if what you perceive is true — even if it contradicts logic.          |
| 8   | **Echo Personality**           | Gain **D8 Advantage** when resisting psionic or emotional control.                                                                | Once per session, the GM may hijack one `Action/Reaction` with a hallucinated or alternate response.                             |
| 9   | **Compulsive Rationalization** | You are unshakable in your self-belief: ignore penalties from guilt, regret, or moral ambiguity.                                  | After any questionable act, you must justify it aloud. If you refuse or fail, take `Stress 1`.                                   |
| 10  | **Ideological Fixation**       | Once per session, you may declare a personal ideology (e.g. "The mission above all") to **gain D10 Advantage** on a related test. | If you violate or compromise that belief, **take `Stress 2`**. If others do, you must confront them — verbally or otherwise.     |

### Reducing Stress

To recover stress naturally the characters must spend some [[structure|downtime]] to rest and relax, allowing them to recover at least one point of stress. Stress recovery can be increased in the following ways:

- Spend the downtime in activity relating to their [[engrams]], to recover additional point per engram engaged with this way
- Spend some time in therapy
- Use drugs or other substances that alleviate stress
- Partake in other relaxing activities
